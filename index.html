When AI Helps (and When It Doesn’t)

Enterprise AI tools excel at drafting, summarizing, brainstorming, and first-pass analysis. They are not a substitute for judgment, original research, or confidential data handling.

✍️

Strong Use Cases

Drafting emails, summarizing long documents or meeting notes, generating outlines, brainstorming lesson ideas, refining language, and creating first-draft tables or lists.

⚠️

Use Caution

Anything involving student data, unpublished research, personnel matters, or legally sensitive information. Always verify facts. Never paste confidential content into unapproved tools.

🔒

Protected Environments

University-approved tools (accessed via SSO) provide stronger data protections. Prefer these over consumer versions for any work-related tasks.

Prompting That Works

Clear context and specific instructions produce far better results. Think of a prompt as a brief to a capable colleague.

Role: You are a helpful academic writing assistant.

Task: Rewrite the following paragraph for clarity and a professional tone while preserving the original meaning.

Constraints: Keep it under 120 words. Use active voice. Do not invent new claims.

Input: [paste your text here]

Give Context

Who is the audience? What is the purpose? What tone do you need?

Be Specific

Request structure, length, format, or examples. Vague prompts get vague answers.

Iterate

Ask follow-up questions. “Make this shorter,” “Add three examples,” or “Explain this more simply.”

Responsible Use Checklist

A quick reference before you hit send or share AI-generated content.

  • ✓
    Verify accuracy. AI can confidently invent facts, citations, or statistics. Cross-check anything important.
  • ✓
    Protect sensitive data. Do not paste student records, unpublished research, personnel information, or credentials into AI tools.
  • ✓
    Disclose when appropriate. Be transparent with colleagues or students when AI substantially contributed to work product.
  • ✓
    Own the final output. You remain responsible for the accuracy, tone, and appropriateness of anything you distribute.
  • ✓
    Use approved tools. Stick to university-supported platforms for work tasks whenever possible.

3-Day Weekend AI Workshop
for Faculty & Staff

A practical, hands-on weekend intensive focused on responsible adoption of enterprise AI tools (Microsoft Copilot, Google Gemini, Claude, and ChatGPT). Designed for mixed-experience groups of non-technical users.

Workshop Overview

Audience: Faculty, staff, and administrators with little to no prior AI experience.
Format: Hybrid or in-person, 4–5 hours per day with breaks.
Goal: Build confidence, establish responsible habits, and leave with immediately usable skills and resources.

Prompting fluency Responsible use Tool selection Workflow integration Peer support model

Facilitator Notes

Keep sessions interactive. Cap lecture segments at 15–20 minutes. Prioritize hands-on time with university-approved tools. Have a “safe sandbox” document ready for exercises so no one needs to use real sensitive data.

Materials Needed

SSO access instructions, sample non-sensitive documents, shared prompt template library, evaluation rubric for participant outputs, and a simple feedback form.

Success Indicators

Participants leave with at least three refined prompts, a personal cheat sheet, a micro-resource for their team, and a 30-day action plan. Post-workshop survey measures confidence and intended use.
